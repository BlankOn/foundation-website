# Debian Day 2026 Jakarta: Jakarta Linux Users Group and BlankOn Foundation Join Forces

---

Debian Day 2026 Jakarta was held on Saturday, August 29, 2026, at the MULA Office, Jakarta. The meetup was put together by the Jakarta Linux Users Group (JKTLUG) and BlankOn Foundation, with BlankOn Foundation as the sponsor.

Debian Day marks the anniversary of the Debian Project, which was founded on August 16, 1993. Every year around that date, communities in various countries hold their own local celebrations, from small meetups to full day events. That is why Debian Day gatherings usually pop up throughout August, including here in Indonesia.

There were two sharing sessions. The first was a chat about Debian derivative works: what it takes to build a distro on top of the Debian base, the responsibilities that come with it, and how a derivative keeps a healthy relationship with its upstream while still offering something of its own. BlankOn, as an Indonesian distro built on Debian, made for a handy example.

![First session of Debian Day 2026 Jakarta on Debian derivative works](/content/assets/debian-day-jakarta-2026-first-session.jpg)

The second session, led by Adi Ginting, was the declaration of the Jakarta Linux Users Group ([jktlug.id](https://jktlug.id)), aiming to be a home for local and global free and open source projects' contributors in Jakarta. The group also carries on the legacy of the Jakarta Linux community that came before it, KPLI Jakarta or LinuxJak. The session mapped out the community's goals, creating a space that's approachable for newcomers, while building a culture that actively encourages members to contribute to upstream and distribution projects and to engage with regional Linux user groups.

![Adi Ginting presenting the declaration of the Jakarta Linux Users Group (jktlug.id)](/content/assets/debian-day-jakarta-2026-second-session.jpg)

After that, the room moved on to snacks, stickers, and a long networking session. This was the part where the conversations flowed most freely, old acquaintances caught up, new faces got introduced around, and a few ideas for the next activity started taking shape.

Games ran straight off a Linux desktop on a big screen, which is a much better answer than any slide to the question people still ask about whether Linux is ready for everyday gaming.
